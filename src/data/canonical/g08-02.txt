DO NOT CONFUSE THE PRODUCT WITH THE PERMISSION
With affiliate marketing, the customer is buying someone else's product. With MRR, PLR, or another license, you may receive permission to sell or modify material, but permission itself is not customer value.

Before choosing either route, keep two questions separate:
1. What useful thing would the customer actually receive?
2. What legal or commercial arrangement allows me to participate in selling it?

THREE DIFFERENT ROLES THAT CAN LOOK SIMILAR FROM THE OUTSIDE

Affiliate or referral selling means you send a customer toward another company's offer and receive compensation when the required attribution rules are met. You do not normally own the product or the customer transaction.

A resale-rights model gives you permission to sell a digital product under defined terms. The rights can vary dramatically. Some licenses allow resale only. Some allow editing or rebranding. Some allow your customer to resell it again. The label is less important than the actual license.

Licensing can also describe broader arrangements where you receive permission to use, distribute, adapt, or commercialize someone else's intellectual property. Again, the agreement defines what you can actually do.

WHY THE DISTINCTION MATTERS

These routes create different kinds of dependence. An affiliate can be affected by a merchant changing its commission or attribution rules. A licensed seller can be affected by restrictions in the license and by how many other people can sell substantially the same material. Someone using PLR may have substantial freedom to transform the content, but that freedom is useful only if the resulting product gives the customer a reason to choose it.

The question is not merely, "Which one lets me make money?" It is, "What would I actually control, what would I be responsible for, and why would the customer buy my version or follow my recommendation?"
