A SIMPLE SIDE-BY-SIDE EXAMPLE

Imagine an existing $50 digital course.

As an affiliate, you might send someone to the creator's checkout page. If the program pays 30 percent on an attributed sale, you receive $15 and the creator remains responsible for delivering the course. You did not buy or own the course.

Under a resale-rights arrangement, you might instead receive a license allowing you to sell copies yourself. Whether you can change the title, edit the content, use your own branding, or pass resale rights to your customers depends on the license.

With PLR, the license may permit substantially more editing and rebranding. That can give you room to create a differentiated version, but the exact rights still come from the license, not from the letters "PLR."

WHY CONTROL AND RESPONSIBILITY MOVE TOGETHER

The more control you have over the thing being sold, the more decisions can become yours. An affiliate can avoid product creation and fulfillment, but has relatively little control over the merchant's product, checkout, pricing, or program terms. A seller with broad licensing rights may control more of the customer offer, but now has more responsibility for making that offer useful and distinct.

Do not choose based only on which model sounds easiest. Decide how much of the product and customer experience you actually want to own.

A FEW RIGHTS TERMS YOU MAY RUN INTO

When people sell existing digital products, several labels get used loosely online. The label matters less than the actual license.

A LICENSE is permission from the rights holder to use something in specified ways. Permission to use a product is not automatically permission to resell it, change it, put your name on it, or give your own customers resale rights.

MRR usually means MASTER RESELL RIGHTS. In many products carrying that label, the buyer receives permission to resell the product and may also be allowed to pass certain resale rights to the next buyer. The exact permission varies.

PLR usually means PRIVATE LABEL RIGHTS. These licenses often allow more modification, such as editing or rebranding the material, but again the actual license controls.

Think of these as permission structures, not guarantees of quality or profitability. Two products both advertised as PLR can give you very different rights.

Before buying a rights-based product, read what you are actually allowed to do, what you are prohibited from doing, and whether the rights you need are included.
