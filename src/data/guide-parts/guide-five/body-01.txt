There are two very different ways to build around a digital product. You can own the product you're selling, or you can earn money from a digital product someone else owns.

Owning the product does not mean you must personally do every piece of writing, design, coding, or production. It means the offer is yours to control. Selling someone else's product means your rights and economics depend on another creator, company, program, or license.

That difference affects how much you need to create, how much control you have, what you can change, how you get paid, and what happens if the other company changes its terms.

IF IT IS MINE
You decide what the product is, who it is for, what it includes, and how it changes. You can hire people or use tools to help create it. Ownership is about control, not doing every task personally.

IF IT BELONGS TO SOMEONE ELSE
You can skip much of the original creation work, but you inherit constraints. Programs change, licenses restrict use, products disappear, and companies change prices or terms.

The question is whether you would rather take on creation in exchange for control or accept more dependence in exchange for starting with something that already exists.
