For example, an affiliate may have almost no control over the product itself. Someone with broad PLR rights may be allowed to edit and rebrand substantial portions of the material. A reseller may have permission to sell a product while having little permission to change it.

So "mine or someone else's?" is not really about whose computer produced the original file. It is about whether you are building the asset customers buy or entering an arrangement around an asset that already exists.

A SIMPLE OWNERSHIP TEST

Ask: "If this digital product disappeared tomorrow, would I be responsible for rebuilding the thing the customer buys?"

If yes, you are probably thinking about creating your own digital product.

If no because another creator or company owns the underlying product and your role is to refer, resell, license, or otherwise participate in its sale, you are probably in the existing-product branch.

This test is not legally definitive. It is simply a useful way to identify which problem you are trying to solve next.
