SIDE-BY-SIDE EXAMPLE
Imagine you want to help new homeowners maintain their houses.

Digital version: a one-time home-maintenance planner, inspection checklist bundle, or digital tracking tool.
Physical version: a branded home-maintenance kit, specialty tool, printed organizer, or another tangible product.

The customer problem can be similar while the work required to create and deliver the solution is completely different.

If you picture yourself building this business, which set of work sounds more appealing to investigate?

DO NOT CHOOSE FROM STEREOTYPES
Digital does not necessarily mean passive, cheap, or high-margin. Physical does not necessarily mean expensive, complicated, or inventory-heavy. The later branches exist because both categories contain very different ways of operating.

FOLLOW THE SAME KNOWLEDGE INTO TWO PRODUCTS

Suppose you know how to help first-time dog owners prepare for the first month with a puppy.

A digital version might be a $29 guide containing preparation checklists, routines, supply lists, troubleshooting information, and printable planning pages. Once the files exist, another customer can receive a copy without you manufacturing another physical unit.

A physical version might be a puppy-preparation kit containing selected supplies, printed materials, storage, or other tangible components. Every additional sale requires another physical kit to exist and reach the customer.
