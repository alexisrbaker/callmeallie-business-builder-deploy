The customer problem can be similar while the business behind the product changes dramatically.

The digital product creates work around creating, updating, hosting, protecting, and delivering information or files. The physical product introduces sourcing or manufacturing, inventory decisions, packaging, shipping, damage, storage, and physical quality.

Digital is not automatically easy or passive. Physical is not automatically complicated or expensive. The point is to notice which kind of work you would rather build around the customer result.

WHAT YOU ARE ACTUALLY DECIDING HERE

Do not choose digital because someone told you it has better margins. Do not choose physical because it feels more "real."

Choose based on which product-building problem you would rather solve.

With digital, ask whether the value you want to create can genuinely be delivered as information, media, files, software, tools, or another nonphysical experience.

With physical, ask whether the customer's desired result depends on receiving an actual object.

Sometimes both are possible. Someone teaching home organization could sell a digital room-planning system or a physical set of organizing products. The better starting branch depends on what you want the customer to buy, not on which category sounds more entrepreneurial.

IF BOTH SOUND POSSIBLE

You do not need to prove that your idea can only exist in one format.

Ask which version you would rather investigate FIRST.

If the core value is knowledge, instructions, calculations, media, files, or functionality that can be delivered without a physical object, start with digital.

If the customer needs to receive an object for the product to do its job, start with physical.

If either could work, choose the version whose creation and delivery you would rather learn about. You can revisit the alternative later.
