If one-off sales appeal to you, the next useful distinction is whether you want to work with something physical or digital. Neither is automatically easier or better. They simply create different kinds of work.

A digital product can usually be delivered without inventory or shipping, and once it exists, the cost of delivering another copy may be very small. That can work well for information, education, templates, creative assets, software, and other electronically delivered tools.

A physical product gives the customer something tangible. Depending on the model, that can mean production, suppliers, inventory, packaging, storage, shipping, returns, or fulfillment. You might make the product yourself, have someone manufacture your version, resell it, dropship it, or simply refer customers to it.

DIGITAL DOES NOT MEAN EASY
Digital products remove inventory and physical shipping, but the value still has to be created. A useful guide needs useful information. A template needs to work. Software needs to perform its job. The advantage is that delivering copy number 100 often does not require creating the product 100 times.

PHYSICAL DOES NOT MEAN YOU HAVE TO MAKE IT
A physical business can involve hand-making products, using a manufacturer, buying wholesale, dropshipping, or referring customers for commission.

A USEFUL TEST
Ignore what sounds more passive or impressive. Picture the work. Would you rather develop information, tools, files, or digital experiences, or work with tangible products, suppliers, production, inventory, or physical fulfillment?
