"Digital product" describes how something is delivered, not what the customer is actually buying. Before choosing a format, it helps to ask what kind of value you want to give them.

Someone who needs an explanation or plan might want a guide. Someone who repeatedly performs a task might benefit from a template, spreadsheet, checklist, or resource. Someone learning a skill may need structured instruction. A creator or business may pay for an asset they can use. In some cases, the useful thing is a digital tool or piece of software.

The goal is not to pick the format that sounds most impressive. It is to match the format to the problem.

DRAFT QUESTIONS
What do people ask you for help with? What have you learned to do? What information, system, shortcut, tool, or resource could save someone time, confusion, or effort? What could you realistically create and maintain?
