A COMMON CONFUSION
One-off describes the purchase, not the lifespan of the product. A customer can use a purchased template for ten years and it is still a one-off purchase. A physical item can wear out and be replaced later and the original transaction can still have been one-off.

WHAT ONE-OFF CHANGES ABOUT THE BUSINESS

When each purchase stands on its own, the business cannot assume that yesterday's customer creates next month's revenue. That changes what you need from the offer. The first purchase has to make sense on its own, and the economics cannot depend on a second payment that may never happen.

That does not mean every sale comes from a brand-new person. A customer who likes one woodworking plan may later buy another. Someone who buys a chair may return for a table. A customer may purchase several templates over time. The important distinction is that each later purchase is another decision, not an automatic continuation of the first transaction.

This is why one-off businesses can look very different from one another. A $12 template, a $300 course, a $2,000 piece of furniture, and a commissioned illustration can all be one-off sales even though the work, margin, customer expectations, and buying process are completely different.
